This is not a reading document. Reference 05 was recognition — you needed to hear those formulas and know what was coming. This one is the opposite: every item here has to come out of your mouth or pen under pressure. Reading it changes nothing.

The mechanism: keep 8–12 items active at a time. Every EE piece must contain at least 4 of them. Every EO recording at least 3. Count them afterwards — it's a number, not an impression. When an item shows up without you planning it, retire it and promote a new one. Nothing else here works if this part doesn't.
Two markers. SUBJ means the item forces the subjunctive — these are worth disproportionate credit because they're the clearest B2 signal in the language, and the most commonly got wrong. CORE marks the twelve items I'd start with if you only took twelve.

Choosing your first twelve — Week 1 active set

Take every CORE item below. They're chosen to spread across categories rather than cluster, so a single EE piece can plausibly contain four of them without sounding like a demonstration. Rotate from Week 2 using your Friday chunk-mining block.

1 · Prendre position — beyond « je pense que »

Section B opens with your position. Leading on je pense que caps you immediately; it's the phrase every candidate uses and it signals nothing about range.

ChunkSenseWhen
À mon sens, … COREto my mindFormal register opener. Safer than selon moi, which is neutral.
Il me semble que + indicatifit seems to meSofter stance. Indicative when affirmative — subjunctive only if negated.
Je suis convaincu que…I'm convinced thatStrong commitment. Section B, when pushing.
Ce qui me frappe, c'est que… COREwhat strikes me isCleft structure — high value, marks focus. Works in both EE and EO.
Je tiens à souligner que…I want to stress thatFormal written. Good in EE Section B body.
Force est de constater que…one has to note thatImpersonal, very formal. Excellent EE conclusion opener.

2 · Nuancer et concéder — the single biggest band marker

This category is where NCLC 6 and 8 separate. A candidate who only asserts reads as limited regardless of vocabulary; a candidate who concedes and then counters reads as competent even with simpler words. If you drill one section, drill this one.

ChunkSenseWhen
Certes…, mais… COREadmittedly… butThe concession move in its purest form. Concede a real point, then counter. Two sentences, enormous credit.
Bien que + subjonctif SUBJ COREalthoughBien qu'il soit difficile de… Never indicative.
Quoique + subjonctif SUBJalthoughInterchangeable with bien que, slightly more literary.
Même si + indicatifeven if / even thoughTrap. Looks concessive, takes indicative. Getting this wrong is a visible error.
Il n'en reste pas moins que + indicatifthe fact remains thatThe counter-move after a concession. Pairs with certes.
Cela dit, …that saidLight pivot. Very natural in EO.
Dans une certaine mesure, … COREto some extentPartial agreement. Buys thinking time in EO.
Sans pour autant + infinitifwithout however doing…sans pour autant négliger… Compact and high-register.
Loin de moi l'idée de…far be it from me toPre-empts a misreading of your position. Section B.

3 · Hypothèse et condition

Conditional structures are explicitly named in NCLC descriptors. You already have the tenses from F6 — this is about having the frames ready, not learning the grammar.

ChunkSenseWhen
Si + imparfait, → conditionnel présent COREif X were, Y wouldPresent unreal. Si j'avais plus de temps, je participerais.
Si + plus-que-parfait, → conditionnel passéif X had been, Y would havePast unreal. Regret or counterfactual — strong B2 signal.
Au cas où + conditionnelin caseTakes conditional, not subjunctive. Au cas où vous seriez disponible…
À supposer que + subjonctif SUBJsupposing thatHypothetical concession in argument.
En admettant que + subjonctif SUBJgranting thatConcede a hypothetical, then dismantle it. Section B.
Quitte à + infinitifeven if it meansQuitte à payer un peu plus… Idiomatic, marks range.

4 · Cause et conséquence

ChunkSenseWhen
Étant donné que… COREgiven thatFormal causal opener. Replaces parce que everywhere in EE.
Dans la mesure où…insofar asQualified cause. High register, very reusable.
Du fait de + nomowing toNominal — compresses a clause into a phrase.
C'est la raison pour laquelle…which is whyRelative-pronoun structure; scores better than donc.
De sorte que + indicatif / subjonctif SUBJso thatMood changes meaning. Indicative = actual result. Subjunctive = intended purpose.
Il en résulte que…the result is thatFormal consequence. EE conclusions.

5 · Reformuler et préciser — your EO safety net

These are worth more in speaking than they look. When a sentence goes wrong mid-way, a reformulation marker turns a breakdown into a deliberate clarification. That's the difference between an error the examiner hears and a move they credit.

ChunkSenseWhen
Autrement dit, … COREin other wordsRestart a failed sentence cleanly.
Je m'explique. CORElet me explainTwo words, buys a full sentence of thinking time. Sounds intentional.
Pour être plus précis, …to be more preciseAdds detail on demand; useful when an answer ran short.
En d'autres termes, …put differentlyWritten register equivalent.
Ce que je veux dire, c'est que…what I mean isCleft again. Spoken, natural, recovers a tangle.

6 · Attribuer et se distancier

Reporting a view without owning it. Directly relevant to EE Section B, where you often summarise a position before disagreeing with it.

ChunkSenseWhen
Selon / D'après + nomaccording toNeutral attribution.
Certains soutiennent que…some maintain thatSets up a counter-argument. Section B staple.
On a tendance à croire que…people tend to believeIntroduces a received idea you'll challenge.
Il paraîtrait que + conditionnelit would appear thatUnverified report. The conditional does the distancing.
Contrairement à ce que l'on pense, …contrary to beliefStrong opener. Note the l'on — formal register marker.

7 · Doser la certitude

ChunkSenseWhen
Il est probable que + indicatifit's likely thatThe contrast is the point. Probable takes indicative; possible takes subjunctive. Using both correctly in one piece is a clear B2 signal.
Il est possible que + subjonctif SUBJit's possible that
Il se peut que + subjonctif SUBJ COREit may be thatCompact and idiomatic.
Rien ne garantit que + subjonctif SUBJnothing guarantees thatNegation forces subjunctive. Counter-argument.
J'aurais tendance à penser que…I'd be inclined to thinkConditional as politeness. Hedged stance.

8 · Structurer — the explicit NCLC 7 marker

Visible intro and conclusion are what separate 7 from 6 in writing. Not vocabulary. Structure. Every EE piece gets an opening frame and a closing frame, without exception.

ChunkSenseWhen
Je me permets de vous écrire au sujet de… COREI'm writing regardingFormal letter opener. Section A.
D'une part…, d'autre part… COREon one hand… on the otherSignals two-part structure. Must actually deliver both.
Non seulement…, mais encore…not only… but alsoInversion follows non seulement at sentence head — check it.
En premier lieu… / En second lieu…firstly / secondlyFormal enumeration, written register.
Pour conclure, / En définitive, COREto conclude / ultimatelyNever end without one of these.
Dans l'attente de votre réponse, je vous prie d'agréer mes salutations distinguées.formal sign-offMemorise verbatim. Free marks, zero thought.

9 · Relances — EO Section A, where the points actually are

Two of the three efficacité criteria can only be earned by reacting to the examiner's answer. A memorised question list structurally caps your score no matter how fluent you sound. These are the phrases that convert an answer into a follow-up.

ChunkSenseWhen
Ah bon ? Et pourquoi cela ? COREreally? why's that?Simplest possible relance. Always available.
C'est-à-dire ?meaning?Two words. Forces elaboration, costs nothing.
Si je comprends bien, … COREif I understand correctlyReformulates their answer — proves you listened. Highest-credit relance.
Vous voulez dire que… ?you mean that…?Checks understanding, invites correction.
Justement, je voulais vous demander…actually, I wanted to askBridges their answer to your next question. Kills the list feel.
Dans ce cas, …in that caseMakes your next question conditional on their answer.
Et concrètement, comment ça se passe ?how does it work in practice?Universal follow-up when you have nothing specific.

10 · Persuader et contrer — EO Section B

The examiner will not agree with you. That's designed. What's scored is whether you keep engaging rather than repeating yourself louder.

ChunkSenseWhen
Je comprends votre point de vue, mais… COREI see your view, butAcknowledge before countering. Every single time.
Vous avez raison sur ce point, cependant…right on that, howeverPartial concession. Reads as confident, not stubborn.
Permettez-moi d'insister.allow me to insistEscalates while staying formal.
Imaginez un instant que + subjonctif SUBJimagine for a momentShifts them into your frame.
Qu'est-ce qui vous retient ?what's holding you back?Turns the objection into information you can answer.
Vous n'avez rien à perdre.you've nothing to loseClosing pressure.
Je vous assure que vous ne le regretterez pas.you won't regret itFinal line. Have it ready.

🇨🇦 Register note. Everything here is standard formal French and correct for TEF Canada. Keep québécismes out of EE and EO entirely — chum, char, magasiner, bienvenue for "you're welcome" are recognition targets from the listening work, not production targets. The exam wants formal register, and it's the same formal register on both sides of the Atlantic.
