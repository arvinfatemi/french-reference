Score, level, NCLC where CLB 7 sits on the TEF scale

The TEF reports each épreuve as a score out of 699 plus a CECR level. IRCC converts that score to an NCLC level. For reading, NCLC 7 starts at 434, which is inside the B2 band. So the job is: never drop an Élémentaire point, win almost every B1 item, and take a good share of the B2 items.

How the CE score is built

  • 40 questions, 60 minutes, on computer. Free navigation: you can go back and change answers (book p. 12).
  • Good answer 1 point, wrong answer 0, no answer 0. Wrong answers aren't penalised, so answer everything (book p. 9).
  • The raw score is converted to 0–699 taking each question's difficulty into account. Which questions you get right matters, not only how many.
  • All six CECR levels are represented in roughly equal shares; A1 slightly less (book p. 9). Difficulty rises through the paper: longer texts, richer language, more precise questions.
  • TEF Canada has four épreuves (CE, CO, EE, EO). The book also covers Lexique et structure, which TEF Canada does not include. Results are valid for two years.
Working target, not an official table. CCI Paris doesn't publish a raw-to-score conversion. If items are spread evenly across A1–C2, a reader at the NCLC 7 threshold gets nearly all A1–B1 items, about two thirds of B2, and a few C1/C2. That works out to roughly 27–31 correct out of 40. The per-section targets in L'épreuve add up to that range.

Method what the book says, plus the trap patterns behind its questions

The rule behind every CE question (book p. 9): all answers are in the document, and the right option is almost always a reformulation of a passage. Wrong options are plausible: partly true with one wrong element, absent from the document, or contradicted by it. Reread the passage before choosing.

Dossiers thématiques the book's texts grouped by theme, plus one new text per theme

The TEF draws from a small set of general-interest themes. Each dossier lists the book documents on that theme, the core B1 vocabulary, the B2 collocations journalists use, and the argument frames you'll see in Parts F and G. I searched for practice material for each CE block, opened each source, and checked its format, level, answer key, volume and price. Sources are linked, not copied. Each one is rated for how closely it matches the TEF block it serves.

Keep two pools. Training is drilling by block: PrepMyTEF exercises, your book's Lexique et structure chapter, the official samples, and one paid course if you want more. Measurement is unseen full papers taken timed in one sitting: the free ATF paper first, TEFCanadaPractice papers if you buy a month, your PrepMyTEF mocks last. Never train on a paper you plan to measure with.
