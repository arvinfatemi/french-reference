Level flag. Everything here is authentic exam register — announcement, broadcast and service-counter formulas that sit above the F6 ceiling. That's deliberate: these are recognition targets, not production targets. You need to hear them and know instantly what's coming, not be able to compose them.

How to use this — the reasoning behind it

Your two failure modes were a critical-mass collapse and losing the opening seconds. These have the same root: French audio gives you no free ride at the start, and if you spend the opening decoding rather than classifying, you never build the frame that holds the rest.

Openings in this exam are formulaic. There are maybe ten genres and each announces itself in its first handful of words with a fixed formula. If you memorise those formulas to the point of instant recognition, the opening seconds stop costing you comprehension and start giving you information — because knowing the genre tells you what slots to expect and in what order. You go from decoding word by word to filling a form you already have.

The move: in the first 3 seconds, don't try to understand. Classify. Ask only "which genre is this?" Once you've answered that, load the slot sequence from that genre's card and listen for slots, not words. Detail you miss inside a known slot is recoverable; detail you miss with no frame at all is gone.

On micros-trottoirs the questions almost never ask what a speaker said — they ask who held which position. Track stance and drop the detail. Trying to hold both is what causes the collapse.

Opening frames by genre — memorise the left column

1 · Message sur répondeur

one voice · no interlocutor · phone quality
  • Bonjour, vous êtes bien sur le répondeur de… — you've reached the voicemail of
  • Vous êtes bien au cabinet / à l'agence… Nous ne sommes pas disponibles pour le moment. — outgoing business greeting
  • Bonjour Madame Leclerc, je vous appelle au sujet de… — formal, caller leaving a message
  • Salut, c'est moi. Je t'appelle juste pour te dire que… — informal register marker
  • Merci de rappeler au… / Veuillez laisser un message après le bip.
1 identity→ 2 motif de l'appel→ 3 date / heure / lieu→ 4 action demandée
Questions target: the action demandée (slot 4) and the changed detail in slot 3. The caller's name is rarely asked — don't burn attention on it.
🇨🇦 Quebec callers often open Ici Marc Tremblay rather than C'est Marc Tremblay. Phone numbers are read in pairs of digits in France, but digit-by-digit in Canada — expect a different rhythm.

2 · Annonce publique

gare · aéroport · magasin · métro · reverb
  • Votre attention s'il vous plaît…
  • Mesdames et messieurs, votre attention…
  • Le train à destination de… partira voie…
  • En raison de…, le départ est retardé de… — delay announcement; the cause comes first
  • Chers clients, notre magasin fermera ses portes dans…
  • Nous invitons les passagers du vol… à se présenter porte…
1 who's affected→ 2 what's happening→ 3 where (voie / porte / rayon)→ 4 when→ 5 consigne
Questions target: slots 3 and 4 — a platform, gate, or time, and very often a change to one. Listen for au lieu de, initialement prévu, exceptionnellement: these mark the answer.
🇨🇦 Montreal metro: Prochaine station…, Attention, les portes se ferment. Stores address Chère clientèle as often as Chers clients.

3 · Publicité radio

music bed · fast · second person
  • Envie de changer d'air ? / Vous rêvez de…?
  • Marre de…? — fed up with (problem-first opening)
  • Nouveau chez… / Découvrez…
  • Profitez de… jusqu'au…
  • Plus que quelques jours pour…
  • Offre valable dans la limite des stocks disponibles. — the small print, always last
1 problem or desire→ 2 product / service→ 3 offer or price→ 4 condition / deadline→ 5 appel à l'action
Questions target: slot 4 almost every time — the restriction, not the offer. Who is this offer NOT for and until when are the classic items. Ads are also the genre where the key figure is repeated at the end, so a missed opening is recoverable here.

4 · Bulletin d'information

studio-clean · measured · third person
  • Il est 8 heures, voici les titres.
  • Bonjour, [nom] avec vous pour ce journal.
  • À la une ce matin…
  • On commence par… / Et puis… — item boundary; a new question usually starts here
  • C'est ce qu'a déclaré… — attributes the quote just given
1 headline→ 2 development→ 3 figure→ 4 quote + source→ 5 transition
Questions target: slot 3 and slot 4 — a number, and who said what. Et puis / On termine avec are your item boundaries: treat each as a reset point where you can re-enter cleanly even if you lost the previous item entirely.

5 · Interview / entretien

two studio voices · long turns
  • Bonjour et bienvenue. Aujourd'hui, nous recevons…
  • Nous sommes avec…, [profession]. Bonjour et merci d'être avec nous.
  • Alors, première question :
  • Justement, vous parliez de… — follow-up marker; a relance, not a new topic
  • Un dernier mot ? — you're at the end
1 guest name + function→ 2 question→ 3 answer→ 4 relance→ 5 closing
Questions target: the guest's function (slot 1) is asked far more often than their name — and it lands in the first five seconds. This is the single genre where losing the opening costs you a guaranteed point. If you catch nothing else at the start, catch the job title.

6 · Micro-trottoir

several short voices · street noise
  • Nous avons posé la question à…
  • Nous sommes allés à la rencontre des…
  • Et vous, qu'en pensez-vous ?
  • Moi, personnellement, je trouve que… — speaker 1 begins
  • Franchement, ça dépend. — the nuanced stance; often a distractor
per speaker: stance→ reason→ personal example
Questions target: matching speaker to position. Build a mental three-column tally — pour / contre / nuancé — and drop everything else. Stance markers do the work: je suis tout à fait pour, ça me dérange pas, je suis plutôt contre, ça dépend des cas.

7 · Conversation de service

two voices · short turns · ambient noise
  • Bonjour, je peux vous aider ? / Qu'est-ce que je vous sers ?
  • Bonjour, je voudrais… / Je viens pour…
  • Je suis désolé, mais… — the problem lands here; the answer is right after
  • Par contre, je peux vous proposer… — the alternative, and usually the correct option
  • Ça vous convient ? / Je vous confirme donc…
1 request→ 2 problem→ 3 alternative→ 4 decision→ 5 confirmation
Questions target: what the customer ends up with, not what they asked for. The first request is nearly always a distractor. Par contre and en revanche are the signal that the real answer is arriving.
🇨🇦 Meal names shift the clock: déjeuner = breakfast, dîner = lunch, souper = dinner. A restaurant booking "pour le dîner" is midday in Quebec and evening in France — this is a live trap on time questions. Also: magasiner (to shop), cellulaire (mobile), bienvenue used for "you're welcome".

8 · Chronique / reportage

single voice · narrative pacing
  • Aujourd'hui, je vous emmène à…
  • Direction [lieu], où…
  • C'est une histoire qui commence il y a…
  • Écoutez plutôt… — hands over to a witness clip
  • Reste une question : — closing open question
1 setting→ 2 main fact→ 3 witness→ 4 figure→ 5 conclusion
Questions target: the journalist's overall stance, and the distinction between what the reporter asserts and what a witness claims. Keep those two voices separate — conflating them is the standard wrong answer.

9 · Annonce d'événement

upbeat · dense with logistics
  • À l'occasion de…
  • Ne manquez pas…
  • Rendez-vous le… à partir de…
  • L'entrée est libre / Tarif réduit pour…
  • Inscription obligatoire avant le…
1 occasion→ 2 what→ 3 date / time→ 4 place→ 5 price / signup
Questions target: slot 5. Who gets the reduced rate, and what the registration deadline is. Listen for sauf, à condition de, dans la limite de.

10 · Message professionnel

workplace · formal · instruction-bearing
  • Bonjour à tous, un petit mot pour vous informer que…
  • Je vous informe que… à compter du…
  • Suite à…, nous vous demandons de…
  • Merci de bien vouloir… — the instruction, politely framed
  • Pour toute question, adressez-vous à…
1 reason→ 2 the change→ 3 consequence→ 4 expected action→ 5 deadline
Questions target: slots 4 and 5 — who must do what, by when. Merci de + infinitive and veuillez + infinitive are both commands, not thanks. Treat them as flashing lights.

If you lose the opening — the re-entry protocol

Do not chase. Reconstructing the first sentence while the audio continues is what turns one lost item into five. Accept the loss, and re-enter at the next structural marker.

French is generous with discourse markers, and they are louder and slower than the content around them. These are your handholds — each one tells you where you are in the schema:

MarkerWhat it tells you
Alors / DoncA conclusion or result is coming. You can re-enter here and still get the point of the passage.
En fait / En réalitéA correction to what was just said. The corrected version is the answer.
Par contre / En revancheContrast. The second half is usually what's asked about.
C'est-à-direA reformulation of something you may have missed — a free second chance at the same idea.
Bref / Pour résumerAn explicit summary. Full re-entry point.
D'ailleursAn aside. Safe to deprioritise.
Et puis / On termine avecItem boundary in a bulletin. Clean reset.
Attention / N'oubliez pasThe consigne is arriving. Highest-value seconds in any announcement.

Three structural facts worth banking:

  • In ads and event announcements, the key figure is repeated at the end. Missing the opening costs you nothing here — wait for the repeat.
  • In announcements, the consigne is always last. If you catch only the final clause, you still get the most-asked item.
  • In interviews, the guest's function is only stated once, at the very start. This is the one genre where the opening is genuinely unrecoverable — so front-load your attention there and nowhere else.

How to drill this — 10 minutes, folded into your listening block

Don't study these cards. Use them as a classification test, in the ten minutes before the 3-pass protocol:

  1. Queue five short clips from different genres.
  2. Play three seconds of each, then stop. Name the genre out loud and recite its slot sequence from memory.
  3. Check against the card. You're scoring classification speed, not comprehension.
  4. When you can classify all five in three seconds each, the opening-seconds problem is solved — move the ten minutes into shadowing instead.

Then run the full 3-pass protocol as normal. During pass 1, consciously name the genre before you start answering. Log whether you named it correctly — that number should hit 100% well before your comprehension scores catch up, and it's the earliest signal that the repair is working.
